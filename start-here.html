Lúba Neamhnithe · Interregnum Nullificans
The Nullification Loop — five stages, zero determinations

Non-recognition is not a decision to reject. It is a decision not to see. A submission that is not recognised need not be evaluated; without evaluation there is no determination; without a determination there is no reviewable act; without a reviewable act there is no basis for judicial challenge. Every stage of the Loop follows from the first move. The same output — no determination — regardless of which department operates the mechanism, which government is in office, or which individual occupies the role. The Loop is personnel-independent. It has run through six Taoisigh and five governments without change of output.

Stage 01
Acknowledgement as Closure
Receipt confirmed. No pathway follows. Correspondence sometimes deleted post-declination.
Stage 02
Remit Displacement
Passed laterally. No body holds the full competence. Jurisdictional evaporation without referral.
Stage 03
Deferral without Destination
Action postponed. No timetable. No accountable actor. Obligation resets on personnel change.
Stage 04
Sufficiency without Audit
Existing arrangements asserted as adequate. No evaluation of the specific submitted corpus furnished.
Stage 05
Record Suppression
Institutional memory does not persist. Files deleted or inaccessible. The loop resets; the matter resurfaces unchanged.

Individual officeholders, once formally notified, are subject to the misfeasance threshold. Knowledge + inaction + harm = tort. The State cannot evaluate what it cannot categorise. O.R. as methodology falls outside existing evaluation grammar. The DTCI was subsequently established to do precisely what the 2006 corpus was designed for — either reinventing, at public expense, a capability in State custody since 2006, or knowingly excluding it. The AI Office, now briefed, operates within the same department that holds the CA designation — and has not resolved the gap. Correction requires both a writ and a curriculum change.
No-Interregnum Axiom · Article 28 · Constitutional Bridge
A statutory obligation is not extinguished by a change of officeholder.

The administrative law principle that obligations travel with functions, not with officeholders, stated with constitutional precision for this record. Its basis is threefold under Bunreacht na hÉireann.

Art. 28.4.1°
Collective Executive Responsibility
The Government is collectively accountable to the Dáil for all executive action. Ministerial rotation and portfolio reallocation are internal arrangements. They do not alter the external legal relationship between the State and a person to whom a statutory obligation is owed. The obligation that crystallised in 2006 did not change character in 2011, 2022, 2024, or 2025. In each case the State remained the obligor; the function changed address; the duty did not lapse.
Art. 28.6
Ministerial Tenure at Taoiseach's Discretion
A minister does not own the function; she holds it on behalf of the State. When she leaves, the function — and any unperformed obligation attached to it — passes to her successor. The Tánaiste who held Foreign Affairs and Defence at the moment of the August 22nd, 2024 CA transfer did not extinguish the unperformed obligation by failing to refer the record to DETE. He passed it, unacknowledged, to a successor architecture that did not know it existed.
High Court Corollary
Mandamus Runs Against the Office, Not the Person
Mandamus is not defeated by a plea that the obligation arose under a predecessor's tenure. The Taoiseach is named in the mandamus application as the head of government in whom the unperformed 2009 ministerial commitment is now personally vested. The argument that a reshuffling of portfolios in the intervening sixteen years extinguishes personal liability is not available where the same individual has held each of the successive offices through which the obligation has passed.
§ 01 — What the law says about this

The tort chain —
joint and several liability

Plain-language guide to what remedies exist and who bears responsibility. From the FLAC strategic litigation brief.

Definition · tort of conversion

Conversion is the wrongful taking, retention, or disposal of another person's property. If you hand your materials to an institution for evaluation, and that institution keeps them for twenty years without evaluating them, returning them, compensating you, or obtaining a licence to hold them — that is conversion. The Civil Liability Act 1961 makes multiple wrongdoers jointly and severally liable: each is fully responsible for the whole harm, not merely their individual share.

I
Mandamus — compel the State to do what it was always required to do

The court can issue an order of mandamus requiring the competent authority to discharge its function under EU Regulation 2021/821 — to actually evaluate the submitted corpus and produce a determination. The primary application compels DETE (the current CA designation-holder) to conduct the evaluation and requires DFA to account for its eighteen-year non-exercise of the CA function. This is not punitive: it is an instruction to do the job the State was required to do twenty years ago.

EU Regulation 2021/821 · what it requires · what Ireland failed to do

Regulation 2021/821 is the EU's primary instrument governing the control of exports, transfer, brokering, and transit of dual-use items. Article 2(1) requires each Member State to designate a competent authority and ensure it functions. Ireland's CA (DFA, 2009–2024) never conducted a single technical evaluation across eighteen years and four transposition cycles. The designation transferred to DETE on August 22nd, 2024 without audit of the accumulated obligation. The current architecture distributes the evaluation function across three institutions with no statutory coordination mechanism — making integrated assessment architecturally impossible. Ireland currently has 46 open infringement cases before the Commission and 6 referred to the CJEU. The Commission's mandatory evaluation report is due no later than September 10th, 2028.
